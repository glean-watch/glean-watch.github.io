The face on the watch

An analog instrument panel: three gauges with scales and needles, four corner readings, a day-date window and hands outlined to read over every gauge.

Mätare is an analog dial built like an instrument panel. Three gauges sit at nine, three and six o'clock, each with its own scale, a filled arc, a needle, an icon and the value: Body Battery, steps and intensity minutes by default, or battery, floors and stress. A needle runs from empty to a goal or a full scale, so a gauge always means something.

Four readings sit in the corners, an icon over its value, from fifteen sources: heart rate, the weather, calories and the next sunrise or sunset by default, or steps, distance, elevation, pressure, notifications and more. At twelve, battery and alerts, and the day and date in a window, in your watch's own language.

The hour and minute hands are outlined in the dial's colour so they read cleanly over a gauge, and a minute track runs round the rim. Every figure is set in Saira.

Five themes: Carbon, Steel, Navy, Racing and Arctic, a white dial. On memory-in-pixel screens every theme is drawn for 64 colours. The always-on mode keeps the hands in outline, the hour markers and the date.
