The face on the watch

Big, easy-to-read numbers over a glowing aurora borealis: the northern lights over snowy mountains, with the date, two readings of your choice and a goal bar.

Aurora borealis watch face with big numbers: the northern lights glowing green and violet over snowy mountain peaks, behind a large, easy-to-read time, the date and the data you check every day.

The time sits on a single line, as wide as the round screen allows, in condensed figures made to be read at a glance: the hours white, the colon and the minutes the colour of your theme. Every figure is drawn over a dark outline, so it stays sharp on the brightest curtain of light.

Above the time, the date in your watch's own language with the seconds ticking beside it, and battery and alerts at the top. Under it, two readings in rounded chips, each with its icon: heart rate and steps to start, or the weather, the day's high and low, Body Battery, stress, calories, distance, floors, sunrise and sunset, elevation, VO2 max, sleep score and more, twenty sources in all. A short bar fills as you meet your steps goal, or floors, intensity minutes, Body Battery, battery or stress.

Four themes from the night sky: Aurora green, Violet, Ice and Snow, or switch the northern lights off for a plain black dial. 12-hour or 24-hour time or as your watch is set. On memory-in-pixel screens the seconds keep running with your wrist down; the always-on mode keeps the time at the same size in a light weight, on black.

Touch and hold a reading or the goal bar to open its glance on your watch, or the date for your calendar. Temperatures follow your watch, or pick °C or °F.
