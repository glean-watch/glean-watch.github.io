The face on the watch

A decorated spruce on a snowy night whose 24 lights fill with your steps, a star that lights at your goal, and the days left to Christmas under the tree.

Gran is a Christmas tree on a snowy night, and every light on it means something. Twenty-four bulbs hang on its garland, and they light one by one as your steps add up, from the lowest branch towards the top. Reach your goal and the star lights. Choose Body Battery, intensity minutes, floors, stress or battery instead, and the tree follows that.

Under the tree, beside the parcels, the days left to Christmas Day, or to Christmas Eve or New Year's Eve if you prefer. On the day itself a star takes the number's place. Next to it, how far the lights have come.

Four readings stand either side of the tree, an icon over each value: heart rate, the weather, Body Battery and the next sunrise or sunset by default, or any of eighteen sources, or none. The date, in your watch's own language, sits at the top with battery and alerts.

Make it yours: five themes (Midnight, Snowfall, Nordic, Gold and Candy), four colours of lights (multicolour, warm white, gold or blue), falling snow light or heavy or none, snow on the branches or not. The time is set in Fraunces, a soft storybook serif, in 12-hour or 24-hour or as your watch is set, with or without a leading zero. The seconds stand small beside it, or switch them off.

On memory-in-pixel screens every theme is drawn for 64 colours. The always-on mode keeps the time, the tree in outline and only the bulbs that are lit, so the gauge still reads with your wrist down.

Touch and hold a reading to open its glance on your watch, or the date for your calendar. Temperatures follow your watch, or pick °C or °F.
